General Objectives

The History syllabus is designed to enable candidates to:

  • Impart knowledge of Nigerian history from earliest times to the present.
  • Identify the similarities and relationships among the peoples of Nigeria as they relate to the issues of national unity and integration.
  • Appreciate Nigerian history as the basis to understand West African and African history.
  • Apply history to understand Nigerian and Africa's relationship with the wider world.
  • Analyse issues of modernization and development.
  • Relate the past to the present and plan for the future.

  1. Land and Peoples of the Nigeria Area

    Contents

    • Geographical zones and the people
    • The people's relationship with the environment
    • Relations and integration among the peoples of different zones

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Identify the geographical zones and their peoples
    2. Establish the relationships between people and their environment
    3. Comprehend the relationships among the various Nigerian peoples

  2. Early Centres of Civilization

    Contents

    • Nok, Daima, Ife, Benin, Igbo Ukwu and Iwo Eleru
    • Monuments and shelter systems (Kuyambana, Durbi-ta-Kusheyi, city walls and palaces)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Examine the significance of the various centres
    2. Establish the historical significance of monuments, caves and rocky formations

  3. Origins and Formation of States in the Nigeria Area

    Contents

    • Central Sudan: Kanuri and Hausa states
    • Niger-Benue Valley: Nupe, Jukun, Igala, Idoma, Tiv and Ebira
    • Eastern Forest Belt: Igbo and Ibibio
    • Western Forest Belt: Yoruba and Edo
    • Coastal and Niger-Delta: Efik, Ijo, Itsekiri and Urhobo
    • Factors influencing origin and migration
    • Social and political organizations
    • Inter-state relations, religion, war and peace

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Relate the different population groups to their traditions of origin
    2. Determine inter-state relations
    3. Account for the social and political organizations

  4. Economic Activities and Growth of States

    Contents

    • Agriculture: hunting, farming, fishing, animal husbandry and horticulture
    • Industries: pottery, salt-making, iron-smelting, blacksmithing, leather-working, wood-carving, cloth-making, dyeing and food processing
    • Trade and trade routes: local, regional, long distance and trans-Sahara trade
    • Expansion of states

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Identify the various economic activities
    2. Differentiate between economic activities and specialties
    3. Relate trade and economic activities to the growth of states

  5. External Influences

    Contents

    • North Africans/Arabs: introduction, spread and impact of Islam
    • North Africans/Arabs: trans-Saharan trade
    • Europeans: early European trade with coastal states
    • Europeans: the trans-Atlantic slave trade (origin, organization and impact)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Assess the impact of North African contact on sub-Saharan peoples and states
    2. Examine the impact of early European coastal contact
    3. Trace the origin, organization and impact of the trans-Atlantic slave trade

  6. The Sokoto Caliphate: The Sokoto Jihad (causes, course and consequences)

    Contents

    • Causes and process of the jihad
    • Establishment and administration of the caliphate and relations with neighbours
    • Achievements and impact of the caliphate
    • Collapse of the caliphate

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Examine the causes and processes of the jihad
    2. Determine the factors leading to the rise of the caliphate
    3. Examine the administrative set-up and relations with neighbours
    4. Examine the impact of the caliphate
    5. Trace the internal and external factors causing its collapse

  7. Kanem-Borno

    Contents

    • Collapse of the Saifawa dynasty
    • Borno under the Shehus
    • Borno under Rabeh

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Determine the factors leading to the collapse of the Saifawa dynasty
    2. Examine the administration of Borno under the Shehus
    3. Assess the historical role of Rabeh

  8. Yorubaland

    Contents

    • Fall of the Old Oyo Empire
    • Yoruba wars and their impact
    • Peace treaty of 1886 and its aftermath

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Examine the causes of the fall of Old Oyo
    2. Examine the causes and effects of the Yoruba wars
    3. Assess the impact of the 1886 peace treaty

  9. Benin

    Contents

    • Internal political development
    • Relations with neighbours
    • Relations with the Europeans

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Examine the internal political development
    2. Examine relations with neighbours
    3. Assess relationships with the Europeans

  10. Nupe

    Contents

    • Internal political development
    • Relations with neighbours

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    Contents

    • Christian missionary activities
    • Activities of trading companies
    • Impact on the coast and hinterland

    1. Examine the motives for interior exploration
    2. Give reasons for the suppression of the slave trade
    3. Trace the development of commodity trade
    4. Examine missionary and European activities
    5. Assess the activities of European trading companies

  17. Colonial Administration After the Amalgamation

    Contents

    • Central administration: Legislative and Executive Councils

    • Local administrative institutions (Native Authorities, Native Courts, Native Treasuries)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Relate the composition of central administration to its consequences
    2. Identify the reasons for the introduction of indirect rule and its workings
    3. Assess the effects of indirect rule
    4. Examine the local administrative units
    5. Account for the anti-colonial movements and their significance

    Contents

    • Infrastructure: transportation, post and telecommunication
    • Agriculture
    • Mining
    • Industry
    • Commerce
    • Banking

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Examine the nature of the economy as it affected taxation, currency, infrastructure, agriculture, mining, industry, commerce and banking

  19. Social Development under Colonial Rule

    Contents

    • Western education
    • Urbanization and social integration
    • Improvement unions
    • Health institutions

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Identify the areas of social development
    2. Examine the impact of urbanization on the people
    3. Examine the level of social integration

  20. Constitutional Developments, Nationalism and Independence

    Contents

    • Rise of nationalist movements
    • 1922 Clifford Constitution and Nigeria's first political party
    • World War II and independence agitation
    • Richards Constitution of 1946
    • Macpherson Constitution of 1951
    • Party politics: regionalism, federalism and minorities agitations
    • Lyttleton Constitution of 1954
    • Constitutional conferences in Lagos 1957 and London 1958
    • General elections of 1959 and independence in 1960

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Trace the emergence of nationalist movements
    2. Assess the roles of the different constitutions in development
    3. Examine the effects of World War II on independence agitation and constitutional developments
    4. Trace the development of party politics and its impact on regionalism and the minority question
    5. Examine the impacts of the constitutional conferences
    6. Determine the factors aiding the attainment of independence

  21. The Politics of the First Republic and Military Intervention

    • Struggle for control of the centre
    • Revenue allocation issues
    • The minority question
    • 1962/63 census controversies
    • Action Group crisis and the 1964/65 general elections
    • January 1966 coup d'etat and the Ironsi Regime

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. Give reasons for the struggle to control the centre
    2. Account for the revenue allocation controversies
    3. Account for the controversies generated by the minority question and state creation
    4. Account for the 1962/63 census controversies
    5. Examine the problems created by the Action Group crisis and the 1964/65 elections
    6. Assess the significance of the military intervention and the Ironsi Regime

    Contents

  42. Problems of Nation-building in Africa

    2. Assess the effects of natural disasters on Africa

Frequently Asked Questions

Has the JAMB History syllabus changed for 2027?
No. JAMB has not published a separate History syllabus for the 2027 UTME. As of 21 November 2026, the History PDF on IBASS, JAMB's syllabus portal, is byte for byte the same 12-page file it served on 27 August 2026. If JAMB replaces it when registration opens, this page and its date change with it.
How many topics does the JAMB History syllabus contain?
There are 42 topics in total, arranged within seven sections across the two parts. Part I (Nigerian History) holds the bulk of them, from Land and Peoples up to Nigeria in International Organizations, while Part II adds the continental and global topics such as the Mfecane, the Great Trek and Apartheid.
