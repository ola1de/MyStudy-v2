General Objectives

The Christian Religious Studies syllabus is designed to enable candidates to:

  • acquire the knowledge and understanding of the tenets of the Christian faith as contained in the Bible
  • interpret biblical teachings and themes
  • apply biblical teachings and tenets to life in society
  • evaluate the level of application of biblical teachings and tenets to life in society

  1. The Sovereignty of God

    Contents

    • God as Creator and Controller of the Universe (Gen. 1 and 2; Amos 9:5-6; Is. 45:5-12; Ps. 19:1-6; Jer. 18:1-16; Rom. 8:28)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. define the term 'sovereignty'
    2. analyse God's process of creation
    3. interpret the sequence of creation
    4. identify man's role in advancing God's purpose in creation
    5. recognize the sovereignty of God in the affairs of man and nations

  2. The Covenant

    Contents

    • The flood and God's covenant with Noah (Gen. 6:1-22; 7:1-24; 9:1-17)
    • God's covenant with Abraham (Gen. 11:31-32; 12:1-9; 17:1-21; 21:1-13; 25:19-26)
    • God's covenant with Israel (Ex. 19; 20; 24:1-11; Deut. 28:1-19)
    • The New Covenant (Jer. 31:31-34; Ezek. 36:25-28)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. explain the concept of covenant
    2. examine the importance and implication of the covenants
    3. distinguish between God's covenants with Noah, Abraham and Israel
    4. distinguish between the old and the new covenants

  3. Leadership Qualities

    Contents

    • Joseph (Gen. 37:1-28; 41:1-57; 45:1-15)
    • Moses (Ex. 1; 2; 3; 4:1-17; 5; 12; Num. 13:1-20; 14:1-19)
    • Joshua (Num. 13:21-33; 27:15-23; Josh. 1:1-15; 6; 7; 24:1-31)
    • Judges: Deborah (Judges 4:1-24), Gideon (Judges 6:11-40), Samson (Judges 13:1-7, 21-25; 16:4-31)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. examine the circumstances that gave rise to the leadership of Joseph, Moses, Joshua and the Judges
    2. identify the major talents of these leaders
    3. assess God's role in the works of these leaders
    4. analyse the achievements of these leaders

  4. Divine Providence, Guidance and Protection

    Contents

    • Guidance (Ex. 13:17-22; Josh. 8:1-22; Heb. 1:1; Matt. 11:27-30)
    • Protection (Ex. 14:10-31; Dan. 6:16-23; Ps. 91)
    • Provision (Ex. 16:1-21; 17:1-7; 13:20-22; 1 Kings 17:1-16; Num. 20:1-13)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the different ways by which God guided and protected the people of Israel
    2. specify how God provided for His people
    3. identify the different occasions when God provided for Israel
    4. recognize that God still provides for people today

  5. Parental Responsibility

    Contents

    • Eli and Samuel (1 Sam. 2:11-36; 3:2-18; 4:10-22; 8:1-5)
    • David (2 Sam. 13; 15:1-29; 18; 19:1-8)
    • Asa (1 Kings 15:9-15; 22:41-44; Deut. 6:4-9; Prov. 4:1-10; 13:1, 24; 22:6; 23:13-14; 31:10-31)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. determine the extent to which Eli, Samuel and David were responsible for the shortcomings of their children
    2. describe how Asa pleased God

  6. Obedience and Disobedience

    Contents

    • Obedience and Rewards: Abraham (Gen. 22:1-19), Three Hebrew Youths (Dan. 3:1-30), David (1 Sam. 30:1-20)
    • Disobedience and Consequences: Adam (Gen. 2:15-25; 3), Collection of Manna (Ex. 16:22-30), The Golden Calf (Ex. 32), Moses (Num. 20:7-12; Deut. 34:1-6), Saul (1 Sam. 10:1-16; 15:1-25; 16:14-23; 31:1-13)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. determine why Abraham, the Three Hebrew Youths and David obeyed God
    2. identify the rewards for obedience
    3. compare the disobedience of Adam, the people of Israel, Moses and Saul
    4. indicate the reasons for their disobedience
    5. identify the consequences of disobedience

  7. A Man After God's Own Heart

    Contents

    • The early life of David (1 Sam. 16:1-13; 17; 18:17-30; 22:1-5; 24:1-23; 2 Sam. 2:1-7; 3:1-39)
    • David's submission to the will of God (1 Sam. 26:1-25; 2 Sam. 12:15-25)
    • David's repentance and forgiveness (2 Sam. 11; 12:1-15; cf. Ps. 51)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify David's anointing experience
    2. specify how David submitted to the will of God
    3. examine the situations that led to David's sin and repentance
    4. identify the consequences of David's sin
    5. identify why God forgave David

  8. Decision-Making

    Contents

    • Reliance on a medium (1 Sam. 28:3-25)
    • The wisdom of Solomon (1 Kings 3:3-28; 4:29-34; 5:1-12; 8:1-53)
    • Unwise policies of Solomon and Rehoboam (1 Kings 9:15-23; 11:1-40; 12:1-20)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the source of Solomon's wisdom
    2. compare the different ways used by Saul and Solomon in making decisions
    3. analyse the decisions made by Saul, Solomon and Rehoboam
    4. assess the consequences of Solomon and Rehoboam's unwise decisions

  9. Greed and Its Effects

    Contents

    • Ahab (1 Kings 21:1-29; 22:1-40; 2 Kings 9:30-37)
    • Gehazi (2 Kings 5:1-27; Josh. 7)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. deduce the meaning of greed
    2. distinguish between Ahab and Gehazi's greed
    3. analyse the consequences of Ahab and Gehazi's greed

  10. The Supremacy of God

    Contents

    • Religious tension and the power of God on Mount Carmel (1 Kings 16:29-34; 17:1-7; 18; 19:1-18)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. assess the religious situation in Israel at the time of Elijah and Ahab
    2. identify the characters involved in the contest on Mount Carmel
    3. differentiate between God's power and that of Baal

  11. Religious Reforms in Judah

    Contents

    • Cleansing of the Temple (2 Kings 22)
    • Renewal of the Covenant (2 Kings 23:1-30)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyse Josiah's religious reforms
    2. determine the reasons for the renewal of the covenant
    3. assess the significance of the reforms

  12. Concern for Judah

    Contents

    • The fall of Jerusalem (2 Kings 24; 25:1-17)
    • Condition of Judah (Neh. 1:1-11; Ezra 1:1-11)
    • Response to the state of Judah (Neh. 2; 4:1-23; Ezra 3; 4; 5; 6; 7)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the reasons for the fall of Jerusalem
    2. examine the condition of Judah during the exile
    3. analyse the people's response to the call of Nehemiah and Ezra to rebuild Jerusalem
    4. distinguish between Nehemiah and Ezra's responses to the opposition of their enemies

  13. Faith, Courage and Protection

    Contents

    • Daniel, Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego (Dan. 3:1-30; 6:1-28)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyse the stories of Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego and Daniel
    2. determine the occasions in which the four men demonstrated faith
    3. analyse the effects of the faith of the four men on the Babylonians
    4. identify the qualities of faith of the four men worthy of emulation

  14. God's Message to Nineveh

    Contents

    • Jonah and his message (Jonah 1; 2; 3; 4)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyse the story of Jonah's call
    2. describe the consequences of Jonah's disobedience
    3. assess the effect of Jonah's message on the Ninevites
    4. emulate the example of the Ninevites

  15. Social Justice, True Religion and Divine Love

    Contents

    • Social justice and true religion (Amos 2:6-8; 4; 5:1-25; 6:1-14; 7:10-17; 8:4-14; James 1:19-27)
    • Divine love and human response (Hosea 1; 2; 3; 4; 6:1-11; 14)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. determine what true religion is
    2. identify the ills that led to the call for social justice in Amos' time
    3. identify the punishment meted for evil deeds
    4. examine the condition in Israel during Hosea's time
    5. analyse Hosea's portrayal of divine love and human response

  16. Holiness and Divine Call

    Contents

    • The calls of Isaiah, Ezekiel and Jeremiah (Isaiah 6:1-13; Ezek. 2; 3:1-11; Jer. 1:4-10)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyse the vision of Isaiah on God's holiness
    2. distinguish the calls of Isaiah, Ezekiel and Jeremiah
    3. compare the assignments given to these prophets
    4. determine the need for God's people to be holy

  17. Punishment and Hope

    Contents

    • Punishment and restoration (Jer. 3:11-18; 32:26-35; Ezek. 18; 37:1-14; Isaiah 61; Jer. 4:5-8)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. describe the situations that led to the punishment of Israel
    2. identify the conditions for hope
    3. determine the benefits of restoration

  18. The Birth and Early Life of Jesus

    Contents

    • John, the forerunner of Jesus (Lk. 1:5-25, 57-66; 3:1-20; 7:18-35; Mk. 1:1-8; 6:14-29; Mt. 3:1-12; 11:2-19; Jn. 1:6-8, 19-37; 3:22-36)
    • The birth and boyhood of Jesus (Mt. 1:18-25; 2; Lk. 1:26-45; 2)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. compare the stories of the births of John and Jesus
    2. assess the importance of John as the forerunner of Jesus
    3. describe the boyhood of Jesus

  19. The Baptism and Temptation of Jesus

    Contents

    • The baptism and temptation of Jesus (Mt. 3:13-17; 4:1-11; Mk. 1:9-13; Lk. 3:21-22; 4:1-13)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. determine the meaning and purpose of the baptism of Jesus
    2. enumerate the temptations of Jesus
    3. examine the significance of the temptations of Jesus
    4. describe how Jesus overcame the temptations

  20. Discipleship

    Contents

    • The call of the first disciples (Mt. 4:18-22; 9:9-13; Mk. 1:16-20; 2:13-17; Lk. 5:1-11, 27-32)
    • The demands of discipleship (Mt. 8:19-22; Lk. 9:57-63; 14:25-33)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the first disciples to be called by Jesus
    2. determine the demands of discipleship

  21. Miracles

    Contents

    • Nature miracles: Stilling the storm (Mt. 8:23-27; Mk. 4:35-41; Lk. 8:22-25), Feeding of the five thousand (Mt. 14:13-24; Mk. 6:30-44; Lk. 9:10-17; Jn. 6:1-13), Walking on the sea (Mt. 14:22-26; Mk. 6:45-52; Jn. 6:16-21), Changing water to wine (Jn. 2:1-11)
    • Miracles of resuscitation: The raising of Lazarus (Jn. 11:1-45), The raising of Jairus' daughter (Lk. 8:41-42, 49-56; Mk. 5:21-43), The raising of the widow's son at Nain (Lk. 7:11-17)
    • Healing miracles: The lepers (Mt. 8:1-4; Mk. 1:40-45; Lk. 5:12-16; 17:11-19), The paralytic at the pool (Jn. 5:1-17), The centurion's servant (Mt. 8:5-13; Lk. 7:1-10), The blind (Jn. 9:1-12; Mk. 10:46-52; Lk. 18:35-43)
    • Exorcism: The Gerasene (Gadarene) demoniac (Mt. 8:28-34; Mk. 5:1-20; Lk. 8:26-39), The epileptic boy (Mk. 9:14-29; Lk. 9:37-43a; Mt. 17:14-21)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. classify the different miracles of Jesus
    2. indicate the occasion of each of the miracles
    3. examine the significance of each of the miracles

  22. The Parables

    Contents

    • Parables of the kingdom: The sower (Mt. 13:1-23; Mk. 4:1-20), The weeds (Mt. 13:24-30, 36-43), The drag-net (Mt. 13:47-50), The wedding garment (Mt. 22:1-14)
    • Parables about love of God (Mt. 18:12-14; Lk. 15:1-32)
    • Parables about love for one another (Lk. 10:25-37; 16:19-31)
    • Parable about wealth: The rich fool (Lk. 12:13-21)
    • Parables on prayer (Lk. 18:2-14)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. define a parable
    2. classify the different parables of Jesus
    3. identify the occasion of each parable
    4. interpret the meaning of each parable
    5. give reasons why Jesus taught in parables

  23. Sermon on the Mount

    Contents

    • The Sermon on the Mount (Mt. 5; 6; Lk. 6:17-26)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyse the teachings on the Mount
    2. identify the demands of the Kingdom
    3. determine the consequences of placing worldly possessions above heavenly treasures
    4. associate the rewards for obedience with the Sermon on the Mount

  24. Mission of the Disciples

    Contents

    • The mission of the twelve (Mt. 10:5-15; Mk. 6:7-13; Lk. 9:1-6)
    • The mission of the seventy (Lk. 10:1-24)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. distinguish between the mission of the twelve and the seventy
    2. specify the instructions to the disciples
    3. assess the outcomes of the missions

  25. The Great Confession

    Contents

    • Peter's confession (Mt. 16:13-20; Mk. 8:27-30; Lk. 9:18-22)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyse the confession by Peter
    2. identify the occasion of the Great Confession
    3. examine the significance of the Great Confession

  26. The Transfiguration

    Contents

    • The Transfiguration (Mt. 17:1-13; Mk. 9:2-13; Lk. 9:28-36)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. trace the events leading to the Transfiguration
    2. determine the significance of the Transfiguration to the disciples
    3. identify the personalities involved in the Transfiguration account

  27. The Triumphal Entry and the Cleansing of the Temple

    Contents

    • The Triumphal Entry and the cleansing of the Temple (Mt. 21:1-17; Mk. 11:1-19; Lk. 19:29-48)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. recount the Triumphal Entry and the cleansing of the Temple
    2. determine the significance of the Triumphal Entry and the cleansing of the Temple
    3. examine how the cleansing of the Temple caused hostility towards Jesus

  28. The Last Supper

    Contents

    • The Last Supper (Mt. 26:17-30; Mk. 14:10-26; Lk. 22:7-23; Jn. 13:2-38)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. trace the story of the Last Supper
    2. evaluate the significance of the Last Supper

  29. The Trials and the Death of Jesus

    Contents

    • The trials of Jesus before the High Priest (Mt. 26:36-75; Mk. 14:53-72; Lk. 22:66-71), Pilate (Mt. 27:11-26; Mk. 15:1-15; Lk. 23:1-5, 13-25; Jn. 18:28-40; 19:1-16), Herod (Lk. 23:6-12)
    • Crucifixion and burial of Jesus (Mt. 27:32-66; Lk. 23:26-56; Mk. 15:16-47; Jn. 19:17-42)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyse the different trials of Jesus
    2. describe the crucifixion and burial of Jesus
    3. deduce the lessons of the death of Jesus
    4. recount the statements of Jesus on the cross

  30. Resurrection, Appearances and Ascension of Jesus

    Contents

    • The resurrection, appearances and ascension of Jesus (Mt. 28:1-20; Mk. 16:1-20; Lk. 24:1-53; Jn. 20:1-31; Acts 1:1-11)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. trace the stories of the resurrection, appearances and ascension of Jesus
    2. compare the personalities involved in the stories
    3. analyse the relevance of the resurrection, appearances and ascension of Jesus

  31. Jesus' Teachings About Himself

    Contents

    • The Bread of Life and the Living Water (Jn. 4:7-15; 6:25-58)
    • The Light of the World (Jn. 1:4-8; 3:19-21; 8:12; 9:1-5; 12:35-36; 1 Jn. 1:5-7)
    • The Door, the Lamb and the Good Shepherd (Jn. 1:29-34; 10:1-18)
    • The True Vine (Jn. 15:1-11)
    • The Resurrection (Jn. 11:25)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyse the different teachings of Jesus about Himself
    2. deduce the reasons for Jesus' teachings about Himself
    3. interpret the meanings of the symbols used by Jesus about Himself

  32. Love

    Contents

    • God's love for man (Jn. 3:16-18)
    • Love for one another (Jn. 13:34-35; 15:12-13; 1 Cor. 13; 1 Jn. 4:7-21)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. describe God's love for man
    2. identify various types of love
    3. specify the ways they can love one another
    4. evaluate the significance of love

  33. Fellowship in the Early Church

    Contents

    • Communal living (Acts 1:15-26; 2:41-47; 4:32-37)
    • Problems of communal living and solutions (Acts 5:1-11; 6:1-6)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the reasons for communal living in the Early Church
    2. identify the problems of communal living and their solutions
    3. examine how communal living helped the growth of the Early Church

  34. The Holy Spirit and the Mission of the Church

    Contents

    • The Pentecost (Acts 1:8; 2:1-41)
    • The mission of the Church (Acts 8:4-40)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. trace the story of the Pentecost
    2. identify the signs associated with the coming of the Holy Spirit
    3. examine the significance of the Pentecost experience
    4. analyse the mission of the Church

  35. Opposition to the Gospel Message

    Contents

    • The arrest and imprisonment of Peter and John (Acts 3; 4:1-22; 5:17-42; 12:1-24)
    • The martyrdom of Stephen (Acts 6:8-15; 7)
    • Persecution by Saul (Acts 8:1-3; 9:1-2; Gal. 1:11-17)
    • Persecution of Paul (Acts 16:11-40; 19:23-41; 21:27-36; 2 Cor. 11:23-33)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. trace the story of the arrest and imprisonment of Peter and John
    2. trace the events that led to the martyrdom of Stephen
    3. describe the role of Saul in the persecution of the Church
    4. evaluate the importance of persecution to the growth of the Church
    5. account for the persecution of Paul

  36. Mission to the Gentiles

    Contents

    • Conversion of Saul (Acts 9:1-30; 22:4-21; 26:9-18)
    • Conversion of Cornelius (Acts 10:1-48)
    • The commissioning and mission of Paul (Acts 13; 14:1-20)
    • The Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15:1-35; Gal. 2:1-21)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. compare the conversions of Saul and Cornelius
    2. analyse the commissioning and mission of Paul
    3. examine the main decisions at the Council of Jerusalem
    4. identify the personalities involved at the Council of Jerusalem
    5. examine the relevance of the main decisions at the Council of Jerusalem
    6. assess Paul's role in the mission to the Gentiles

  37. Justification by Faith

    Contents

    • Justification by faith (Rom. 3:21-24; 5:1-11; 10:1-13; Gal. 2:16-21)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. interpret the phrase 'justification by faith'
    2. identify the basic conditions for justification
    3. determine the fruits of justification

  38. The Law and Grace

    Contents

    • The Law and Grace (Rom. 4:13-25; 5:18-21; Gal. 3:10-14, 19-29; Rom. 3:24)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. examine the purpose and significance of the law and grace
    2. identify the place of the Law among the Jews

  39. New Life in Christ

    Contents

    • New Life in Christ (Rom. 6:1-4, 12-14; 12; Col. 3:1-17; Gal. 5:16-26; 2 Cor. 5:16-19; 1 Thess. 4:1-8)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. describe the characteristics of the old life
    2. analyse the new life in Christ
    3. identify the conditions of the new life
    4. examine the benefits of the new life

  40. Christians as Joint Heirs with Christ

    Contents

    • Christians as joint heirs with Christ (Gal. 3:23-29; 4:1-7)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. describe how Christians are joint heirs with Christ
    2. recognize that males and females are all children of God
    3. indicate the benefits of being joint heirs with Christ

  41. Humility

    Contents

    • Humility (Phil. 2:1-11; 1 Pet. 5:5-11; James 4:10; Matt. 23:12)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. determine the meaning of humility
    2. identify the requirements of humility
    3. identify the rewards of humility

  42. Forgiveness

    Contents

    • Forgiveness (Philemon; 2 Cor. 2:5-11; Matt. 6:14-15)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyse Paul's teaching on forgiveness
    2. assess the benefits of forgiveness
    3. recognize the need to forgive others

  43. Spiritual Gifts

    Contents

    • Spiritual gifts (1 Cor. 12; Rom. 12:3-18; 1 Cor. 14)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the different spiritual gifts
    2. analyse the benefits of spiritual gifts to the individual and the church
    3. differentiate between spiritual gifts and talents

  44. Christian Giving

    Contents

    • Christian giving (Phil. 4:14-20; 2 Cor. 8:1-5; 9; Matt. 6:2-4)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. interpret the concept of Christian giving
    2. relate the teachings of Paul on Christian giving
    3. identify the importance of Christian giving

  45. Civic Responsibility

    Contents

    • Civic responsibility (Rom. 13; 1 Tim. 2:1-4; 1 Pet. 2:13-17)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the need for obedience to authority
    2. specify the requirements of good citizenship

  46. Dignity of Labour

    Contents

    • Dignity of labour (Matt. 20:1-16; 2 Thess. 3:6-15; Col. 3:23-25)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. interpret the concept of dignity of labour
    2. analyse the benefits of labour
    3. recognize that no particular kind of work leads to loss of respect for personal dignity

  47. The Second Coming of Christ

    Contents

    • The signs of the Coming of Christ (1 Thess. 4:13-18; 2 Thess. 2:1-12)
    • Preparation for His coming (Matt. 25:31-46; 1 Thess. 5:1-11; 2 Pet. 3:1-13)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the signs of the Second Coming of Christ
    2. specify the preparations for His coming
    3. indicate what will happen during His Second Coming
    4. examine the importance of His coming

  48. Impartiality

    Contents

    • Impartiality (James 2:1-13; Acts 10:34-35; Matt. 7:1-5; Lk. 6:31)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. interpret the concept of impartiality
    2. identify causes of partiality
    3. examine the consequences of partiality

  49. Effective Prayer

    Contents

    • Effective prayer (James 1:2-8; 4:1-3; 5:13-18; Matt. 6:5-13)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the requirements of effective prayer
    2. distinguish between effective and ineffective prayer
    3. identify the importance of prayer
    4. identify types of prayer

  50. Christian Living in the Community

    Contents

    • Interpersonal relationships among Christians (1 Pet. 5:1-4; Rom. 12:3-21; 2 Pet. 1:3-11; Heb. 13:1-21)
    • Christians living among non-Christians (1 Pet. 2:3-25; Rom. 15:1-2)
    • Christian attitude to persecution (1 Pet. 1:5-9; 4:1-19; 3:13-22)
    • Relationship in the Christian family (Eph. 6:1-9; Col. 3:18-21; 1 Pet. 3:1-7)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. determine interpersonal relationships among Christians
    2. analyse Christian living among non-Christians
    3. relate Christian attitude to persecution
    4. determine the relationship in the Christian family
    5. examine the importance of maintaining good relationships

  51. Corruption

    Contents

    • Corruption (1 Tim. 6:6-11; 2 Tim. 3:8; 2 Pet. 1:4-11; James 5:1-6)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. define the term corruption
    2. identify the causes of corruption
    3. determine the effects and consequences of corruption
    4. identify ways of curbing corruption

  52. Sexual Immorality

    Contents

    • Prostitution (1 Cor. 6:16-20; Prov. 7:10-27; 23:27-28)
    • Adultery and Fornication (Heb. 13:4; Eph. 5:3-10; Matt. 5:28-32; Deut. 22:22; Lev. 20:10)
    • Homosexuality (Rom. 1:24-32; Lev. 18:21-30; 20:13)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify what constitute sexual immorality
    2. determine the causes of sexual immorality
    3. examine the effects and consequences of sexual immorality
    4. identify ways of curbing sexual immorality

Frequently Asked Questions

Has the JAMB Christian Religious Studies (CRS) syllabus changed for 2027?
No. JAMB has not published a separate Christian Religious Studies syllabus for the 2027 UTME. As of 21 November 2026, the Christian Religious Studies PDF on IBASS, JAMB's syllabus portal, is byte for byte the same 12-page file it served on 27 August 2026. If JAMB replaces it when registration opens, this page and its date change with it.
How many topics are in the JAMB Christian Religious Studies syllabus?
The syllabus contains 52 topics arranged across four sections. Section A (Creation to the Division of the Kingdom) has 8 topics, Section B (Division of the Kingdom to the Return from Exile and the Prophets) has 9 topics, Section C (the four Gospels and the Acts of the Apostles) has 19 topics, and Section D (selected Epistles) has 16 topics.
