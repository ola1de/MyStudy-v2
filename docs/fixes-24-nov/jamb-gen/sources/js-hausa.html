JAMB Hausa Syllabus

General Objectives

The Hausa syllabus is designed to enable candidates to:

  • acquire the ability to read and write competently in the Hausa language
  • know the basic features of Hausa language (syntax, phonology, morphology and semantics)
  • have the basic knowledge of oral and written Hausa literature
  • have the ability to appreciate the culture, customs and institutions of the Hausa people
  • have the ability to translate competently from English to Hausa

  1. Ƙa’idojin Rubutu (Orthography)

    Contents

    • Alphabetization
    • Spelling
    • Rules of word merger and division
    • Punctuation
    • Paragraphing

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. recognize basic orthographical rules
    2. apply these rules in practice
    3. detect linguistic errors (grammar, word choice, spelling)

  2. Auna Fahimta (Comprehension)

    Contents

    • Contextual questions from unseen passages (approximately 200 words)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. read written Hausa texts proficiently
    2. comprehend given texts
    3. interpret varied meanings and word functions
    4. build sufficient vocabulary
    5. recognize central issues
    6. draw conclusions from textual evidence

  3. Tsarin Rubutun Insha’i (Composition Techniques)

    Contents

    • Composition and letter writing techniques in Hausa
    • Composition segments
    • Composition types: argumentative, descriptive, dialogue, expository, narrative and letter writing

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. acquire knowledge of composition writing techniques
    2. recognize different composition segments
    3. identify various composition types

  4. Fassara (Translation)

    Contents

    • Translation types
    • Translation rules
    • Translation problems
    • Translation techniques

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. translate proverbial and idiomatic expressions and new vocabulary from English to Hausa
    2. recognize different translation types
    3. master basic translation techniques for idioms and new terms
    4. identify translation challenges specific to Hausa

  5. Tsarin Sauti (Phonology)

    Contents

    • Consonants (production and classification by phonation, place and manner of articulation)
    • Vowels (production, classification, monophthongs, diphthongs)
    • Tone (high, low, falling)
    • Syllable structure (open/closed, light/heavy syllables; monosyllabic/disyllabic words)
    • Vowel length (long and short)
    • Phonological processes: assimilatory (palatalization, labialization, vowel harmony) and non-assimilatory (insertion, deletion)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyze sound production and sound combinations forming meaningful words
    2. evaluate vowel importance in determining meaning
    3. distinguish phonetic attributes of sounds
    4. recognize syllable counts and types
    5. analyze phonological processes

  6. Ƙirar Kalma (Morphology)

    Contents

    • Roots and stems
    • Affixation (prefix, infix, suffix; derivational and inflectional functions)
    • Gender and number inflections
    • Noun and adjective derivation from verbs; verb and adjective derivation from nouns

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. explain the roots and stems of Hausa words
    2. explain inflectional processes in word formation
    3. explain derivational processes
    4. differentiate between morphological processes

  7. Ginin Jumla (Syntax)

    Contents

    • Word classes (nouns, pronouns, verbs, adverbs, adjectives, prepositions, conjunctions, interjections, ideophones)
    • Grammatical categories: tense and aspect (general and relative past, general and relative continuous, first and second future, habitual); mood (subjunctive and negative); gender (masculine, feminine, neuter); number (singular, plural)
    • Sentence structure: verbal sentence (nominal phrase + verbal phrase and their components); non-verbal sentence (nominal phrase + stabilizer; nominal phrase + complement + stabilizer; nominal phrase + continuous frame (yana../yake…) (+da) + nominal phrase)
    • Sentence types (simple, compound, complex)
    • Clauses (relative and subjunctive types; main and subordinate functions)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyze word combination processes forming phrases, clauses and sentences
    2. detect grammatical errors
    3. recognize written ideas and thoughts
    4. construct meaningful communicative sentences
    5. apply appropriate tenses in spoken and written Hausa
    6. apply appropriate gender and number agreement
    7. differentiate sentence structure types
    8. distinguish nominal from verbal phrases
    9. distinguish between types of sentences
    10. compare clause types

  8. Ma’ana (Semantics)

    Contents

    • Lexical word meaning aspects (ambiguity, synonymy, antonymy)
    • Figures of speech (specialized meanings of words and phrases, e.g. idioms, proverbs)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyze meaning generation mechanisms
    2. build vocabulary
    3. recognize varied sentence meanings and communication functions
    4. select purpose-appropriate vocabulary and sentences
    5. construct meaningful communicative sentences

  9. Rayuwar Hausawa (Hausa Customs)

    Contents

    • Haihuwa (birth): ɗaukar ciki, goyon ciki, haihuwa, shayarwa, al’adun makon farko na haihuwa, wanka, ɓanti, yaye, kaciya, samartaka
    • Marriage (types, courtship, betrothal, wedding, marital life, divorce, widowhood)
    • Mutuwa (death): faɗar mutuwa, wanka, salla, jana’iza, zaman makoki, sadaka, takaba, gado
    • Modern influences on Hausa life practices

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. explain customs from conception through initiation
    2. explain modern birth practices
    3. explain courtship through widowhood customs
    4. explain modern marriage influences
    5. explain death notification through inheritance
    6. explain modern death practice influences
    7. compare modern impact types and meanings

  10. Zamantakewa (Social Institutions)

    Contents

    • Tsarin zaman iyali, zaman gandu, dangantakar kishiyoyi, ‘yan uwantaka, barantaka, agolanci
    • Maƙwabtaka
    • Aikin gandu da na gayya
    • Friendship and companionship
    • Greetings and hospitality
    • Modern influences on social institutions

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. explain family relationship patterns and interactions
    2. explain social institution importance among Hausa peoples
    3. compare modern influence varieties on social customs

  11. Sana’o’in Gargajiya (Traditional Occupations)

    Contents

    • Types: noma, ƙira, jima, kasuwanci, wanzanci, sassaƙa, farauta, dukanci, saƙa, kitso, rini, fawa, fafar ƙorai, da sauransu
    • Yanayinsu: hanyoyin gadon su, kayayyakin yin su, matakan tafiyar da su, muhimmancinsu
    • Manufactured goods and products
    • Sarautunsu (occupational titles)
    • Sana’o’i masu dangantaka da jinsi: aikatau da ƙwadago; kitso da aski
    • Modern occupation influences

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the characteristics of the practitioners
    2. identify the goods produced
    3. distinguish men’s and women’s occupations
    4. describe the tools of the occupations
    5. identify how the occupations are inherited
    6. describe the uses of the products
    7. explain the titles of the occupations
    8. explain the importance of the occupations
    9. compare modern impact on traditional occupations

  12. Kayayyakin Buƙatun Rayuwa (Material Culture)

    Contents

    • Household needs (clothing, indoor furnishings)
    • Other needs: gine-gine, girke-girke, da sauransu
    • Modern influences on material culture

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. distinguish Hausa life necessity items
    2. distinguish necessity item uses
    3. explain modern influences on Hausa material culture

  13. Bukukuwa da Wasanni (Hausa Festivities and Recreational Activities)

    Contents

    • Na addini (religious): bikin salla, takutaha (sallar gani), cika-ciki, saukar karatu
    • Na gargajiya (traditional): kalankuwa, buɗar dawa, bikin shan kabewa
    • Na sana’a (occupational): bikin kamun kifi, dambe, kokawa, wasan farauta, wasan maƙera, hawan ƙaho
    • Na nishaɗi: sukuwa
    • Na yara (children’s games): shalle, kulli-kurciya, a-sha-ruwan-tsuntsaye, gaɗa, carafke
    • Modern festival influences

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. explain Hausa festival and game varieties
    2. demonstrate their significance
    3. explain preservation methods against loss
    4. explain conduct procedures
    5. explain modern festival influences

  14. Camfe-Camfe da Bauta (Traditional Beliefs and Worship)

    Contents

    • Beliefs (camfe-camfe): kangida, camfi, kambun baka, maita
    • Worship (bauta): bori, tsafi
    • Modern influences on traditional beliefs and worship

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. distinguish belief and practice varieties and implementation methods
    2. explain their utility
    3. explain their limitations
    4. explain modern influences

  15. Sarautun Gargajiya (Traditional Authority)

    Contents

    • Types: sarakuna, hakimai, dagatai, masu unguwanni
    • Na bayin sarki: shantali, jakadiya, baraya, da sauransu
    • Religious office holders: liman, alƙali
    • Official functions
    • Modern influences on traditional authority

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. categorize authorities by position type
    2. describe the ranks of the offices
    3. differentiate official duties
    4. explain each position's importance
    5. explain modern influences on them

  16. Magungunan Gargajiya (Traditional Medicine)

    Contents

    • Traditional types (na gargajiya): sassaƙe-sassaƙe, saiwoyi, na gari, na ruwa
    • Religious types (na addini): layu, rubutu, ɗibbu, duba
    • Application methods: sha, shafawa, surace, turare, shaƙawa, taunawa, tsotsawa, ɗaurawa, liƙawa, ratayawa
    • Tsarin amfani da su: awo da kimantawa
    • Functions: riga-kafi da warkarwa
    • Modern influences

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. classify medicine varieties
    2. explain application methods
    3. demonstrate utility
    4. explain modern influences

  17. Zuben Baka (Oral Narratives)

    Contents

    • Prescribed oral-literature text (prose): Mu Sha Dariya — Tofa, B. O. (NNPC, Zaria, 2018)
    • Story types (folktales, legends, narratives, tales, history)

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. distinguish narrative types
    2. employ contextually appropriate narrative vocabulary

  18. Maganganun Azanci (Folk-Sayings)

    Contents

    • Take, kirari, habaici, zambo, karin magana, kacici-kacici, salon magana, adon magana, da sauransu

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. distinguish figures of speech and explain usage methods
    2. interpret and employ contextually appropriate saying vocabulary

  19. Waƙoƙi na Baka (Oral Songs)

    Contents

    • Prescribed oral-literature text (poetry): Jagoran Nazarin Waƙar Baka — Gusau, S. M. (Benchmark Publishers Ltd, 2003)
    • Types: na fada, na jama’a, na maza, na sha’awa, da sauransu
    • Turke (jigo), salo, zubi, tsari, mawaƙi, kayan kiɗa, abin da aka waƙe
    • Waƙoƙin aiki (work songs): na niƙa, daɓe, daka, talla, da sauransu
    • Waƙoƙin yara (maza da mata): na aure, na dandali, da sauransu

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. distinguish oral song varieties
    2. distinguish singers by type
    3. separate musical form, rhythm, structure, arrangement and performance materials
    4. identify the types of work songs
    5. identify those who perform work songs
    6. distinguish the forms of children’s songs

  20. Wasannin Kwaikwayo na Gargajiya (Traditional Drama)

    Contents

    • Prescribed oral-literature text (drama): Wasannin Tashe — Umar, M. B. (NNPC, Zaria, 2009)
    • Na yara: langa, wasan ’yartsana, tashe, wasan gauta, dokin kara, da sauransu; how they are performed; their importance
    • Na manya: ‘yan kama, kalankuwa, hoto, wowwo, tashe, da sauransu; how they are performed; their importance

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. identify the forms of traditional drama
    2. identify how traditional drama is performed
    3. bring out the importance of traditional drama
    4. explain the difference between children’s and adults’ drama

  21. Zube (Written Prose)

    Contents

    • Prescribed text: Maraya — Mahe, I. A. (Extension Publishers Limited, Ibadan, 2021)
    • Narrative form, structure, theme and style

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyze narrative form, structure, theme and style within prose texts
    2. apply orthographical rules during prose reading
    3. interpret word and sentence meanings for comprehension
    4. identify key messages and make evaluative judgments

  22. Waƙa (Written Poetry)

    Contents

    • Prescribed text: Waƙoƙin Wayar Da kai Don Manazarta Hausa Na Ɗaya — Anwar, A. (ABU Press, Zaria, 2024)
    • Form, style, theme and structure of poetry

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. comprehend form, style, theme and structure during poetry analysis
    2. apply orthographical rules particular to poetry
    3. interpret word and sentence meanings during poetry study
    4. connect vocabulary and sentence usage to poetic messages
    5. identify key messages and make evaluative judgments

  23. Wasan Kwaikwayo (Written Drama)

    Contents

    • Prescribed text: Malam Zalimu — Gidan Dabino, A. H. (Gidan Dabino Publishers, Kano, 2013)
    • Setting, form, style, characters and structure of written drama

    Objectives — candidates should be able to

    1. analyze dramatic setting, form, style, characters and structure of written drama
    2. apply orthographical rules for meaning extraction
    3. interpret word meanings for comprehension
    4. identify key messages and make evaluative judgments

Frequently Asked Questions

Has the JAMB Hausa syllabus changed for 2027?
No. JAMB has not published a separate Hausa syllabus for the 2027 UTME. As of 21 November 2026, the Hausa PDF on IBASS, JAMB's syllabus portal, is byte for byte the same 10-page file it served on 27 August 2026. If JAMB replaces it when registration opens, this page and its date change with it.
How many topics does the JAMB Hausa syllabus cover?
There are 23 topic areas in all: eight under Harshe (Language), eight under Al'adu (Culture), and the remainder under Adabi (Literature) covering oral narratives, folk-sayings, songs and drama plus three prescribed written texts (prose, poetry and drama).
